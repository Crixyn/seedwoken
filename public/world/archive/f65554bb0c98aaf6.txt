VAELORA
THE LIVING WORLD
Vaelora is an ocean-bearing living world of six major continents, vast seas, mountain crowns, volcanic provinces, fragmented archipelagos, rain-shadow interiors, polar wilderness, and ecosystems that have spread into nearly every environment capable of sustaining them.
From orbit, the world is familiar enough to understand and strange enough to know it is not Earth. Deep blue and blue-green oceans surround continents shaped by their own tectonic history. Warm shallow seas shift through turquoise, jade, and restrained violet-blue. Great river systems divide green interiors; island arcs trace active crust; glaciers occupy high ranges and polar ground; immense weather systems carry water and heat across the globe.
Six Environmental Giants
Vaelora's six continents are not single-biome territories. Each contains many climates shaped by latitude, elevation, mountains, currents, and distance from the sea. Their present working identities describe their largest environmental signatures rather than permanent names.
The six are now known as Elaris, the Mainland; Sahrel, the Sunscar; Caelune, the Crown; Vulkara, the Broken Furnace; Thyrra, the Thousand Shores; and Nythrune, the Veiled Wilds. Their strongest identities range from great river country and deserts to high mountains, active volcanism, fragmented seas, and ancient hypercompetitive wilderness. None is a single-biome territory.
A Planet That Participates
Vaelora is more than the place where life happens. Its ground, water, deep interior, and living systems carry the unexplained phenomenon called Vael. Whether this reflects consciousness, a planetary biological network, something divine, or something for which no useful category yet exists remains unresolved.
Most of the time, geology remains geology, rain remains weather, and roots still survive by water, minerals, light, and chemistry. Vael is not an excuse for every mystery. Its clearest intervention is rarer and far more consequential: when Vael enters the roots of a selected seedling and the Kindling begins.
Life grows upon Vaelora. Life awakens because of it.

THE SIX CONTINENTS OF VAELORA
Vaelora's continents are environmental giants rather than single-biome territories. Latitude, elevation, currents, geology, rain shadows, and distance from the sea create many ecosystems within each landmass. Elaris is called the Mainland for cultural and historical reasons, not because it must be the physically largest continent.
ELARIS — THE MAINLAND
The temperate and civilizational heartland of the currently known world. Elaris combines broad forests, grasslands, wetlands, freshwater lakes, river valleys, foothills, colder northern forests, and warmer southern regions. Its relative environmental balance has supported extensive movement, settlement, study, and exchange, though its wilderness is by no means harmless.
The Vaelorin is Vaelora's greatest known river, descending from the mountainous interior and crossing much of Elaris before opening into an immense wetland delta. Settlements, migration routes, pollinator corridors, and major ecosystems gather around it. The Silren, Mossrun, Avenwater, the Briarwash, and Orin's Fork are important tributaries and regional rivers without rivaling the Vaelorin's planetary significance.

SAHREL — THE SUNSCAR
A dry continent whose geology remembers a wetter age. Ancient river valleys, fossil lake beds, salt flats, canyon systems, underground aquifers, oasis ecosystems, dry grasslands, and elevated desert plateaus reveal landscapes repeatedly transformed by water and its absence.
The Sunscar is Sahrel's immense arid basin, enclosed by high plateaus and marked by severe day-night temperature swings. Rare rains can awaken dormant seed banks across enormous areas, briefly turning apparently barren country into explosive fields of growth and flowering.

CAELUNE — THE CROWN
Vaelora's great high-altitude continent. Mountain forests rise into cloud forests, plateaus, alpine meadows, exposed rock, glaciers, glacial lakes, and cold desert. Isolated valleys support lineages separated by formidable terrain.
The Crownspine is a colossal system of converging mountain ranges. At its greatest convergence rises Mount Aevor, Vaelora's highest known mountain: a distinctive split crown of gigantic summit horns surrounding an ancient central glacial basin. Caelune's greatest killers are often ordinary physics—altitude, exposure, avalanche, crevasse, and violent weather.

VULKARA — THE BROKEN FURNACE
One of Vaelora's two extreme-danger continents. Vulkara occupies a violently active tectonic province of volcanoes, earthquakes, ashfall, geothermal fields, fumaroles, hot springs, acidic and mineral-rich soils, unstable ground, and young volcanic islands. Destruction and recolonization are routine ecological pressures.
The Cinderwake is the great active volcanic belt crossing Vulkara and continuing offshore through island arcs. It corresponds broadly to the prominent volcanic region visible on the canonical orbital cover. Vulkara is dangerous because the ground itself is unstable.
THYRRA — THE THOUSAND SHORES
An ancient continental mass fragmented by rifting and rising seas until the distinction between continent and archipelago became argumentative. Large islands, peninsulas, reefs, shallow and inland seas, drowned valleys, tidal wetlands, coastal cliffs, tropical forests, mangrove analogues, marine meadows, and inland highlands create extraordinary ecological isolation and exchange.
Its vast fragmented coastal realm is called the Thousand Shores; the number is poetic, not a survey result. Thyrra is a natural future center for studying aquatic and amphibious Phyvera and corresponds broadly to the turquoise archipelagos and shallow seas visible on the canonical cover.
NYTHRUNE — THE VEILED WILDS
Vaelora's second extreme-danger continent, and the opposite of Vulkara. Its ancient stable landscapes allowed biological competition to intensify over immense spans of time. Multilayer forests, parasitic plants, complex fungal networks, wetlands, enormous root systems, defensive vegetation, and coevolved predators make life itself the principal hazard.
The Veil is a vast forest-wetland complex whose canopies stack into multiple ecological worlds, leaving portions of the ground almost untouched by direct sunlight. Somewhere deeper lies the Firstroot. Reports disagree whether it is an organism, a geological structure consumed by roots, a site of unusual Vael activity, a myth, or something for which the Codex has no category. For now, it remains unresolved.
Polar Vaelora
Vaelora has substantial permanent ice, polar land, islands, glaciers, and sea ice, but no continent exists merely to serve as an Antarctica analogue. Polar environments instead occupy the extreme reaches of existing continental systems, including portions associated with Caelune and Thyrra. This preserves the frozen terrain visible from orbit while allowing the six continents to remain ecologically distinct.
Two Ways to Die
In Vulkara, the ground tries to kill you. In Nythrune, everything growing on it might beat the ground to it.

AWAKENING
Vaelora's planetary mystery becomes biologically consequential through Vael, the Kindling, and the developmental pathway that produces Phyvera. This section separates physical geography from the mechanisms of awakening.
The Kindling
A Kindling is the single, irreversible event in which Vael enters a selected rooted seedling or sapling and diverts it into Phyveran development. It happens once. It does not sputter, reconsider, or ask for a second attempt.
To a witness, the event begins below or within the roots as a bioluminescent pulse. Light branches through the root network, converges at the root crown, then races upward through vascular tissue, stem, leaves, and finally the growing tip. When the light fades, the plant may look nearly ordinary. It is not.
If a Kindling begins, awakening has succeeded; the organism can still later die from injury, disease, drought, or other ordinary causes. Why Vaelora Kindles one seedling and leaves another rooted in an ordinary botanical life is one of the central unanswered questions of the world.
Seedform
After Kindling, the plant redirects development toward a rooted enclosed structure called a Seedform—colloquially, an egg. It is not laid. It is grown. Shoots fold inward, protective tissues thicken, and the established root network continues supplying water and minerals while a mobile Phyveran body develops within.
A Seedform should resemble the developmental logic of its plant lineage rather than an animal egg with leaves glued to it. In Rosavyn, that means something closer to a rose bud, gall, seed pod, and tightly folded cane growth. At emergence, parts of the original root system become incorporated into the young Phyveran's rootlike feet.
Rosavyn Seedform Morphology
The canonical Rosavyn Seedform is compact, rooted, and roughly ovoid, but it is not an animal egg. Living rose canes and protective botanical layers curve upward and inward around the developing organism, producing a form reminiscent of a tightly closed rose bud, gall, seed pod, and folded cane growth. The roots visibly transition into the base and remain the Seedform's active connection to soil, water, and minerals.
The Seedform is not a container surrounding a separate creature. The entire structure is part of the developing Rosavyn. Future refinements should favor more tightly closed protective layers, folded young cane tissue and bud-scale-like growth, while keeping defensive structures anatomically faithful to rose prickles rather than oversized fantasy thorns.
Veyra Emergence
The Kindling initiates Veyra expression. During the transforming Kindled-seedling phase, subtle pigmentation hints may appear in new growth. By the mature Seedform stage, the future Rosavyn's Veyra is reliably visible to parents and other observers.
The Seedform doesn't conceal the Veyra. It announces it.
Veyra changes tissue pigmentation, not Seedform anatomy. Rosavyn Seedforms therefore share the same fundamental construction across Pale Thorn, Sage, Golden Cane, Roseblush, Mauve, Copper, Mahogany, and Umber. Pigmentation must remain biologically integrated into living tissues, with plausible gradients and secondary colors rather than paint, shell coating, flat uniform color, or persistent magical glow.
The currently accepted Roseblush reference establishes the foundational Seedform morphology. Its generated measurements and incidental card text are not canonical unless separately approved, and the design may be refined later without discarding its core biological construction.

PHYVERA SPECIES  •  ENTRY 001
001 — ROSAVYN
Rose-line Phyvera  •  Botanical ancestry: Rosa spp.
A rose learned to walk. Predictably, it kept the prickles.
Field Profile
What Makes a Rosavyn a Rosavyn?
Rosavyn are not people wearing botanical accessories. Their bodies are the rose. Intertwined living canes form the frame; older load-bearing tissues lignify into textured wood while younger growth stays smoother, greener, and often red with anthocyanin. Pinnately compound, serrated leaves emerge from believable growth nodes. Curved prickles arise from cane tissue. Flowers are costly reproductive structures, not permanent jewelry.
Sex & Bloom
Rosavyn share one body plan, but mature floral placement creates a clear secondary sexual signal. Females develop the dominant bloom at the crown, where it reads almost like living floral hair without ceasing to be a real flower. Males carry the dominant bloom on a shoulder, shoulder blade, or upper back, visually broadening the silhouette like a botanical echo of musculature. Placement can be asymmetric; nature has never been famous for using a ruler.
The Prickle Trick
Rosavyn can voluntarily extend and retract their prickles into specialized cane tissue. Young individuals possess the structures but do not yet have full control. Mastery arrives around a future-defined maturation threshold analogous to puberty or coming of age; the exact age remains deliberately unsettled. Retraction makes close social contact and movement through dense growth safer. Extension restores the rose's ancient argument: please do not grab me.
Veyra
Veyra is the Phyveran term for an individual's dominant inherited body-tissue pigmentation expression. It is analogous to a pigmentation phenotype, not a race, sex, subspecies, ecotype, or flower color. A Veyra can shift in appearance across young shoots, old wood, scars, joints, seasons, and other tissues without becoming a different Veyra.
Floral Expression
Flower color is inherited separately from Veyra. Ordinary Rosavyn may bloom white or ivory, blush and pink, coral, salmon or apricot, yellow and golden, orange, scarlet, crimson, burgundy, lavender or mauve, near-black red, and natural gradients or bicolors. True blue is not an ordinary rose trait; a genuinely blue Rosavyn bloom would be a meaningful Phyveran mutation or event, not a paint swatch.
Seasonal Life
Spring — Awakening: Dormant buds break, reddish young shoots appear, and activity rises.
Early Summer — Bloom: Foliage, fragrance, flowers, and pollinator activity reach their peak.
Late Summer / Autumn — Fruiting: Successful flowers may develop rose hips; growth slows while resources are stored.
Winter — Dormancy: Foliage can diminish or fall depending on lineage and climate. Woody structure dominates and movement slows.
Ecology & Behavior
Rosavyn are defensive and territorial more often than aggressive. When threatened they can contract their prickle-bearing limbs, hook or entangle with flexible canes, and protect rather than chase. Bees and other pollinators are ecological partners, not decorative particle effects. Fragrance, flower posture, pigmentation, and condition can also signal health, stress, and reproductive state.
Resource allocation tells a story. Favorable light, water, nutrients, and low threat pressure favor leaves, flowers, fragrance, and fruit. Sustained stress favors denser cane, lignification, defense, and reduced flowering. Two genetically similar Rosavyn can therefore grow into visibly different adults without anyone needing to invent a 'battle skin.'
Genetics & Variation
Inherited ranges may affect flower color, petal number, fragrance chemistry, growth habit, mature size, foliage pigmentation, prickle density, hip size, disease resistance, and cold or drought tolerance. Environment modifies expression within plausible limits. Ecotypes and subspecies require real ecological or biological differences; color alone does not qualify.
Reproduction: Seeds Before Miracles
Rosavyn reproduction remains botanical at its core. Flowers, pollen, fertilization, rose hips, and seeds matter. Pair bonds and love can exist independently of reproduction, and pollinators may participate in both biology and culture. Successful reproduction can produce rose hips carrying genetically inherited seeds.
Those seeds are not guaranteed children in the walking sense. They germinate as plants. Some may live their entire lives as rooted roses; a seedling becomes Rosavyn only if Vaelora Kindles it. A family lineage may therefore include both mobile Rosavyn and rooted rose descendants, making ancestral gardens quite literally family gardens.
Exact male/female reproductive roles are intentionally unresolved. Real roses often contain both male and female floral structures, so Rosavyn reproduction will be designed from botanical biology rather than forcing mammalian mechanics onto a rose.
Biological Realism & Sex Differences
Earth roses usually bear bisexual flowers, so Rosavyn male/female identity is treated as a Vaeloran post-Kindling specialization rather than a direct copy of Rosa reproduction. The established crown-bloom female and shoulder/back-bloom male pattern remains a secondary sexual signal. Future reproductive canon should preserve real rose structures—stamens, carpels, pollination, hips, and seeds—while allowing the two sexes to bias reproductive investment differently. Retractable prickles remain an explicit fantasy adaptation: real rose prickles are fixed epidermal/cortical outgrowths, so Rosavyn control requires specialized living attachment tissue and carries a tissue/energy cost.
Signature Talent — Briarhold
Under severe threat, a Rosavyn can redirect stored resources into rapid extension and interlocking of existing young canes, forming a temporary living briar enclosure. It costs water and stored carbohydrates. Repeated use can suppress flowering, exhaust the individual, or cause cane dieback. Heroics, regrettably, still require calories.
Vulnerabilities
Fire becomes especially dangerous as woody tissue accumulates. Drought limits regeneration and bloom. Persistent fungal disease can strip vigor from the whole organism, heavy shade reduces growth, and overinvestment in defense steals resources from reproduction and fruiting.
Developmental Stages
Rosavyn development begins as ordinary Rosa-line life: Seed → Germination/Seedling. Most unkindled seedlings continue as ordinary rooted plants. A selected seedling experiences the Kindling, then passes through Transforming Kindled Seedling → Seedform → Infant → Mid Stage → Adult. The accepted Seedform, Infant, Mid Stage, and Adult references now anchor the developmental direction; ordinary germination, the Kindling event, and the transforming seedling remain to be visually established.
Official Visual Record
Rosavyn visual canon now includes Official Image Card 001 for the adult male and female, plus accepted Mid Stage female, Mid Stage male, Infant male, and Infant female reference cards. Together they establish a continuous developmental direction from infancy through adulthood. Female dominant blooms develop at the crown/head; male dominant blooms develop at the shoulder, shoulder blade, or upper back. Seedling remains the only major Rosavyn developmental stage not yet visually established. Earlier exploratory Rosavyn generations remain non-canon.

Solavyr
SPECIES 002 • SUNFLOWER-LINE PHYVERA
Classification: Phyvera
Botanical ancestry: Helianthus spp.; sunflower-line.
Core identity: Height, sunlight, endurance, pollination, reproductive investment, resilience, and ecological coexistence.
Adult scale: Males approximately 6–8 ft; females approximately 5.5–7.5 ft, with substantial individual overlap.
Biology & Ecology
Solavyr are mobile sunflower-derived Phyvera built from reinforced herbaceous stalk anatomy rather than woody tree tissue. Their bodies retain longitudinal vascular structure, coarse botanical trichomes, growth nodes, large functional Helianthus leaves, and compact root-derived feet. They are not primarily attack organisms: defense favors tough fibrous tissues, deterrent chemistry, trichomes, stability, and endurance. Their ecology is strongly pollinator-associated, and long-established Solavyr populations may influence nearby plant communities through the chemistry of shed tissues and root-associated compounds.
Light Orientation & Rest
Young Solavyr retain strong heliotropic and circadian repositioning. Adults retain exceptional awareness of sunlight direction, day length, sunrise, and seasonal change without continuously tracking the sun. Night rest is typically a stabilized standing or partially crouched brace-rest: root-feet spread for support, foliage lowers, activity decreases, and reproductive structures remain protected.
Sexual Dimorphism
Sex is primarily distinguished by reproductive architecture rather than human-like coloration. Adult males carry a predominantly forward-facing, upright capitulum associated with pollen presentation and pollinator exposure. Adult females carry a backward-facing, slightly upward/oblique capitulum on a separate reinforced peduncle emerging from the upper back. The female peduncle is physically separate from the neck and built to support increasing seed mass. As seeds mature, the capitulum may progress from backward/upward to backward and finally backward/downward. Canonical female anatomy also includes a fuller paired botanical chest structure, a substantial load-bearing build, and somewhat narrower, more elegant functional leaves.
Biological Realism Note
The Solavyr sex system is derived Seedwoken biology, not ordinary sunflower reproduction. Earth Helianthus heads contain many florets and commonly include fertile bisexual disc florets. In Solavyr, the Kindling is proposed to canalize that ancestral reproductive machinery into sex-biased capitula: males invest more heavily in pollen presentation, while females invest more heavily in ovule, seed, and load-bearing capitulum development. This preserves recognizable sunflower floral anatomy while making adult sex differences biologically functional.
Signature Defensive Talent — Sunbrace
Under severe physical stress, a Solavyr can redistribute tension through reinforced fibrous tissues, spread its root-derived feet, lower its center of gravity, and stiffen major vascular/fiber bundles. Sunbrace improves resistance to wind and impact but can cause tissue strain, temporary rigidity, and vascular damage if overused.

Tomavyn
SPECIES 003 • TOMATO-LINE PHYVERA
Classification: Phyvera
Botanical ancestry: Solanum lycopersicum; tomato-line.
Status: Established species; active biological and visual development.
Core identity: Herbaceous living plant anatomy, chlorophyll-rich structural tissue, mature red carotenoid-rich storage corridors, glandular trichomes, true tomato-leaf morphology, and biologically integrated fruit-line traits.
Biology & Visual Direction
Tomavyn are tomato-derived Phyvera whose bodies remain living herbaceous plant tissue rather than bark-armored humanoids. Green photosynthetic and structural tissues dominate the body, while mature red tissues are integrated through vascular and storage regions rather than appearing as attached tomatoes, exposed muscle, paint, or decoration. Fine glandular trichomes and recognizable tomato foliage remain important ancestry markers.
Adult Sexual Dimorphism
Tomavyn visual dimorphism can include broader shoulders, a wider upper torso, and greater green-tissue dominance in the established adult male, but these are secondary traits rather than the biological definition of sex. Earth tomato flowers are normally bisexual/perfect, so Tomavyn male and female forms represent a Vaeloran post-Kindling reproductive specialization. Proposed male reproductive clusters emphasize viable pollen production with reduced ovary investment; proposed female clusters emphasize stigma/ovary function and seed-bearing reproductive structures after fertilization, with reduced pollen investment. Both sexes retain recognizable tomato floral ancestry and glandular trichomes. The species remains herbaceous: no bark armor, no exposed-muscle look, and no decorative tomatoes embedded in the body.
Canonical / Accepted Visual Record
Tomavyn adult visual development is established in the Seedwoken archive, including the accepted adult male direction and its front, side, back, portrait, tissue, leaf, and surface-detail studies. Further lifecycle, reproductive, ecological, Veyra, and regional canon should remain open until explicitly accepted.

Lilavyn
SPECIES 004 • LILAC-LINE PHYVERA • CONCEPT
Classification: Phyvera
Botanical ancestry: Syringa vulgaris; common-lilac line.
Status: LOCKED CONCEPT — accepted as the fourth Seedwoken species direction; detailed biology remains provisional until developed.
Concept identity: Fragrance, clustered spring bloom architecture, flexible multi-stem growth, pollinator relationships, seasonal expression, and regenerative/suckering potential.
Concept Foundation
Lilavyn are the fourth Seedwoken species concept, following Rosavyn, Solavyr, and Tomavyn. Their design direction is lilac-derived and should remain grounded in real Syringa biology: opposite simple leaves, terminal flower panicles, fragrant tubular flowers, multi-stem shrub growth, and the lineage's ability to produce basal or root suckers.
Proposed Biological Direction
The accepted concept explores fragrance as meaningful biological signaling, dense reproductive flower clusters rather than decorative blossoms, pollinator-linked ecology, seasonal changes in flowering and resource allocation, and unusually strong vegetative recovery. These are concept-level directions, not finalized mechanisms. Future development must determine anatomy, reproduction, sexual dimorphism, defensive adaptations, lifecycle morphology, Veyra, ecology, and geographic placement before those details become canon.
Sex Differences & Biological Realism
Common lilac is a woody, multi-stemmed shrub with opposite simple leaves and terminal panicles of fragrant tubular flowers. Its flowers are ordinarily bisexual, so Lilavyn sexes are a derived Seedwoken specialization rather than a literal Syringa trait. Proposed males develop pollen-biased panicles with proportionally stronger anther output and somewhat lighter reproductive loading; proposed females develop ovule/fruit-biased panicles and invest more strongly in fertilized capsules and seed maturation. Fragrance remains important in both sexes and can vary with reproductive state. Vegetative recovery should use realistic basal/root suckering and woody regrowth rather than instant regeneration.
Visual Concept Record

The image itself incorrectly labels the species as “Species 002”; that numbering is superseded. Lilavyn is Species 004. The concept is accepted, while individual anatomy, color variants, lifecycle depictions, region, and written biological claims shown on the exploratory poster remain provisional unless separately confirmed.

Irivyn
SPECIES 005 • IRIS-LINE PHYVERA • LOCKED CONCEPT
Classification: Phyvera
Botanical ancestry: Iris spp.; iris-line, with exact ancestral clade still open.
Status: LOCKED CONCEPT — foundational visual direction accepted; detailed region, Veyra, abilities, and lifecycle morphology remain provisional.
Concept identity: Rhizome/bulb-line persistence, sword-like foliage, highly structured iris flowers, seasonal renewal, and strong environmental sensitivity.
Biology Foundation
Irivyn should preserve the defining architecture of Iris: herbaceous perennial tissues; long, strap-like or sword-shaped leaves; flowers organized around three outer falls and three inner standards; and underground storage/renewal organs that may be rhizomes, corms, or bulbs depending on the eventual ancestral lineage. The concept may favor a rhizomatous wet-meadow or waterside lineage, but “iris” does not automatically mean aquatic: many Iris species occupy ordinary meadows, slopes, or comparatively dry habitats. Any final habitat must match the chosen ancestry.
Sex Differences
Most Earth irises have flowers containing both pollen-producing and ovule-producing organs. Irivyn male/female forms therefore represent a post-Kindling specialization. Proposed males bias floral investment toward prominent anthers, pollen production, and display structures; proposed females bias investment toward receptive stigmatic tissue, ovary development, capsule formation, and seed provisioning. External differences may be subtle: flower orientation, reproductive-organ proportions, body resource storage, and seasonal condition should identify sex more reliably than human-like body shape alone.
Biological Limits
Rhizome-based regeneration can support clonal recovery and replacement shoots, but it is not instant healing. Severe rhizome damage, rot, drought, nutrient shortage, or loss of photosynthetic foliage should remain meaningful threats. Bioluminescence, supernatural water sensing, and other poster ideas are not yet canon and require separate biological justification before acceptance.
Visual Concept Record
Irivyn Concept Reference 001 — accepted September 9, 2026 as Species 005 concept direction. The adult silhouettes and iris-derived floral language are retained as the visual foundation. Poster-specific Veyra names, region assignment, ecological role, bioluminescence, and exact abilities remain proposals.

Lilloryn
SPECIES 006 • TRUE-LILY-LINE PHYVERA • LOCKED CONCEPT
Classification: Phyvera
Botanical ancestry: Lilium spp.; true-lily line.
Status: LOCKED CONCEPT — replaces the rejected Nymphara and Mycelith Species 006 directions.
Concept identity: Bulb-stored resilience, upright herbaceous growth, six-tepal floral architecture, fragrance/pollinator signaling, seasonal dormancy, and elegant but structurally functional foliage.
Biology Foundation
Lilloryn are true-lily-derived Phyvera and should be built from herbaceous perennial anatomy rather than aquatic water-lily anatomy. Their ancestry is anchored by underground bulbs, erect stems, simple leaves with parallel venation, and large flowers composed of six tepals with six stamens surrounding a central style/ovary system. Many Lilium flowers are fragrant and pollinator-associated. Their bodies may favor moist valleys, lake margins, or sheltered fertile ground, but the species is not inherently aquatic or semi-aquatic. Persistent waterlogging is a biological liability because true-lily bulbs are vulnerable to rot in poorly drained soil.
Sex Differences
Earth Lilium flowers normally combine stamens and pistil in the same flower, so Lilloryn sex differences are a Vaeloran specialization created after the Kindling. Proposed males allocate more reproductive tissue to elongated stamens, versatile anthers, pollen quantity, and fragrance/display used to attract pollinators. Proposed females allocate more tissue to stigma receptivity, the central style/ovary system, three-part capsule development, seed provisioning, and bulb reserves needed to recover after reproduction. Females may therefore carry slightly heavier lower-torso/bulb-derived storage anatomy, while males may show more conspicuous pollen-bearing floral structures. Both remain unmistakably the same species, and some individuals can be visually difficult to sex outside flowering season.
Growth, Defense & Limits
The bulb is a major reserve organ and can support seasonal dieback followed by regrowth, giving Lilloryn strong recovery across seasons without making them rapidly regenerative. Damage that destroys the basal plate, major bulb scales, or enough stored carbohydrate can be fatal. Floral tepals are display and reproductive structures, not armor; defensive adaptations should instead arise from toughened vegetative tissue, chemistry, posture, or other separately developed mechanisms. Any future toxicity must be defined carefully from a chosen Lilium lineage rather than generalized from the plant’s well-known danger to particular Earth animals.
Visual Concept Record
Lilloryn Concept Reference 001 — accepted September 9, 2026 as Species 006. The latest lily-derived adult male/female concept is the foundational visual direction. Its wetland scenery is aesthetic/environmental exploration, not evidence that Lilloryn are aquatic. Poster Veyra names, healing roles, water-filtration claims, and rapid tissue-regeneration claims are not canon unless later developed and approved.

BIOLOGICAL REALISM STANDARD
Seedwoken uses Naturalistic Biological Fantasy: approximately 70% recognizable biology and 30% fantasy adaptation. Each Phyvera should preserve meaningful traits of its Earth-plant ancestry—growth form, tissues, leaves, roots or storage organs, vascular logic, flowers, pollination, fruit/seed structures, chemistry, seasonality, ecological relationships, and real limitations. Fantasy traits should normally be extensions of those systems with costs and constraints. When an ancestral Earth plant has bisexual flowers but the Phyvera has male and female individuals, the Codex must explicitly identify that split as a post-Kindling Vaeloran specialization rather than falsely claiming the Earth plant is naturally dioecious. Visual sexual dimorphism should follow reproductive investment and plant architecture first; human-like proportions may exist as secondary traits but cannot be the sole biological explanation.
