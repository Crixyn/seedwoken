

VAELORA
THE LIVING WORLD
Vaelora is an ocean-bearing living world of six major continents, vast seas, mountain crowns, volcanic provinces, fragmented archipelagos, rain-shadow interiors, polar wilderness, and ecosystems that have spread into nearly every environment capable of sustaining them.
From orbit, the world is familiar enough to understand and strange enough to know it is not Earth. Deep blue and blue-green oceans surround continents shaped by their own tectonic history. Warm shallow seas shift through turquoise, jade, and restrained violet-blue. Great river systems divide green interiors; island arcs trace active crust; glaciers occupy high ranges and polar ground; immense weather systems carry water and heat across the globe.
Six Environmental Giants
Vaelora's six continents are not single-biome territories. Each contains many climates shaped by latitude, elevation, mountains, currents, and distance from the sea. Their present working identities describe their largest environmental signatures rather than permanent names.
One is dominated by immense humid forests, wetlands, river basins, and a branching living delta. Another holds a colossal collision-built mountain system surrounding elevated interiors. A tectonically restless continent carries volcanic arcs, geothermal country, mineral-rich valleys, and young islands. Elsewhere, a great mountain spine creates an extreme rain shadow: wet forests give way to grasslands, scrub, salt basins, and immense dry country. An ancient fractured continent dissolves toward its margins into peninsulas, shallow seas, reefs, drowned valleys, and archipelagos. At the pole, a pale continent combines glaciers with dark mountains, seasonal tundra, ice-free valleys, and geothermal refuges.
A Planet That Participates
Vaelora is more than the place where life happens. Its ground, water, deep interior, and living systems carry the unexplained phenomenon called Vael. Whether this reflects consciousness, a planetary biological network, something divine, or something for which no useful category yet exists remains unresolved.
Most of the time, geology remains geology, rain remains weather, and roots still survive by water, minerals, light, and chemistry. Vael is not an excuse for every mystery. Its clearest intervention is rarer and far more consequential: when Vael enters the roots of a selected seedling and the Kindling begins.
Life grows upon Vaelora. Life awakens because of it.

WORLD ROOTS
Vaelora
Vaelora is the living world beneath every Seedwoken root: planet, ground, water, deep interior, and—depending on whom you ask—creator. Seedwoken cultures revere Vaelora with a devotion humans might recognize as religious, although the Codex refuses to pretend the metaphysics are settled.
Whether Vaelora consciously chooses, dreams, responds through a planetary biological network, or represents something stranger remains unknown. Scientists can observe what the world does. Priests, poets, gardeners, and particularly confident relatives will happily explain why.
Vael
Vael is the name given to the unknown life-bearing phenomenon carried through Vaelora's ground, water, and living systems. It is detectable most dramatically during the event that creates a Phyveran life, but its origin and full nature remain unresolved.
The Kindling
A Kindling is the single, irreversible event in which Vael enters a selected rooted seedling or sapling and diverts it into Phyveran development. It happens once. It does not sputter, reconsider, or ask for a second attempt.
To a witness, the event begins below or within the roots as a bioluminescent pulse. Light branches through the root network, converges at the root crown, then races upward through vascular tissue, stem, leaves, and finally the growing tip. When the light fades, the plant may look nearly ordinary. It is not.
If a Kindling begins, awakening has succeeded; the organism can still later die from injury, disease, drought, or other ordinary causes. Why Vaelora Kindles one seedling and leaves another rooted in an ordinary botanical life is one of the central unanswered questions of the world.
Seedform
After Kindling, the plant redirects development toward a rooted enclosed structure called a Seedform—colloquially, an egg. It is not laid. It is grown. Shoots fold inward, protective tissues thicken, and the established root network continues supplying water and minerals while a mobile Phyveran body develops within.
A Seedform should resemble the developmental logic of its plant lineage rather than an animal egg with leaves glued to it. In Rosavyn, that means something closer to a rose bud, gall, seed pod, and tightly folded cane growth. At emergence, parts of the original root system become incorporated into the young Phyveran's rootlike feet.
Rosavyn Seedform Morphology
The canonical Rosavyn Seedform is compact, rooted, and roughly ovoid, but it is not an animal egg. Living rose canes and protective botanical layers curve upward and inward around the developing organism, producing a form reminiscent of a tightly closed rose bud, gall, seed pod, and folded cane growth. The roots visibly transition into the base and remain the Seedform's active connection to soil, water, and minerals.
The Seedform is not a container surrounding a separate creature. The entire structure is part of the developing Rosavyn. Future refinements should favor more tightly closed protective layers, folded young cane tissue and bud-scale-like growth, while keeping defensive structures anatomically faithful to rose prickles rather than oversized fantasy thorns.
Veyra Emergence
The Kindling initiates Veyra expression. During the transforming Kindled-seedling phase, subtle pigmentation hints may appear in new growth. By the mature Seedform stage, the future Rosavyn's Veyra is reliably visible to parents and other observers.
The Seedform doesn't conceal the Veyra. It announces it.
Veyra changes tissue pigmentation, not Seedform anatomy. Rosavyn Seedforms therefore share the same fundamental construction across Pale Thorn, Sage, Golden Cane, Roseblush, Mauve, Copper, Mahogany, and Umber. Pigmentation must remain biologically integrated into living tissues, with plausible gradients and secondary colors rather than paint, shell coating, flat uniform color, or persistent magical glow.
The currently accepted Roseblush reference establishes the foundational Seedform morphology. Its generated measurements and incidental card text are not canonical unless separately approved, and the design may be refined later without discarding its core biological construction.

001 — ROSAVYN
Rose-line Phyvera  •  Botanical ancestry: Rosa spp.
A rose learned to walk. Predictably, it kept the prickles.
Field Profile
What Makes a Rosavyn a Rosavyn?
Rosavyn are not people wearing botanical accessories. Their bodies are the rose. Intertwined living canes form the frame; older load-bearing tissues lignify into textured wood while younger growth stays smoother, greener, and often red with anthocyanin. Pinnately compound, serrated leaves emerge from believable growth nodes. Curved prickles arise from cane tissue. Flowers are costly reproductive structures, not permanent jewelry.
Sex & Bloom
Rosavyn share one body plan, but mature floral placement creates a clear secondary sexual signal. Females develop the dominant bloom at the crown, where it reads almost like living floral hair without ceasing to be a real flower. Males carry the dominant bloom on a shoulder, shoulder blade, or upper back, visually broadening the silhouette like a botanical echo of musculature. Placement can be asymmetric; nature has never been famous for using a ruler.
The Prickle Trick
Rosavyn can voluntarily extend and retract their prickles into specialized cane tissue. Young individuals possess the structures but do not yet have full control. Mastery arrives around a future-defined maturation threshold analogous to puberty or coming of age; the exact age remains deliberately unsettled. Retraction makes close social contact and movement through dense growth safer. Extension restores the rose's ancient argument: please do not grab me.
Veyra
Veyra is the Phyveran term for an individual's dominant inherited body-tissue pigmentation expression. It is analogous to a pigmentation phenotype, not a race, sex, subspecies, ecotype, or flower color. A Veyra can shift in appearance across young shoots, old wood, scars, joints, seasons, and other tissues without becoming a different Veyra.
Floral Expression
Flower color is inherited separately from Veyra. Ordinary Rosavyn may bloom white or ivory, blush and pink, coral, salmon or apricot, yellow and golden, orange, scarlet, crimson, burgundy, lavender or mauve, near-black red, and natural gradients or bicolors. True blue is not an ordinary rose trait; a genuinely blue Rosavyn bloom would be a meaningful Phyveran mutation or event, not a paint swatch.
Seasonal Life
Spring — Awakening: Dormant buds break, reddish young shoots appear, and activity rises.
Early Summer — Bloom: Foliage, fragrance, flowers, and pollinator activity reach their peak.
Late Summer / Autumn — Fruiting: Successful flowers may develop rose hips; growth slows while resources are stored.
Winter — Dormancy: Foliage can diminish or fall depending on lineage and climate. Woody structure dominates and movement slows.
Ecology & Behavior
Rosavyn are defensive and territorial more often than aggressive. When threatened they can contract their prickle-bearing limbs, hook or entangle with flexible canes, and protect rather than chase. Bees and other pollinators are ecological partners, not decorative particle effects. Fragrance, flower posture, pigmentation, and condition can also signal health, stress, and reproductive state.
Resource allocation tells a story. Favorable light, water, nutrients, and low threat pressure favor leaves, flowers, fragrance, and fruit. Sustained stress favors denser cane, lignification, defense, and reduced flowering. Two genetically similar Rosavyn can therefore grow into visibly different adults without anyone needing to invent a 'battle skin.'
Genetics & Variation
Inherited ranges may affect flower color, petal number, fragrance chemistry, growth habit, mature size, foliage pigmentation, prickle density, hip size, disease resistance, and cold or drought tolerance. Environment modifies expression within plausible limits. Ecotypes and subspecies require real ecological or biological differences; color alone does not qualify.
Reproduction: Seeds Before Miracles
Rosavyn reproduction remains botanical at its core. Flowers, pollen, fertilization, rose hips, and seeds matter. Pair bonds and love can exist independently of reproduction, and pollinators may participate in both biology and culture. Successful reproduction can produce rose hips carrying genetically inherited seeds.
Those seeds are not guaranteed children in the walking sense. They germinate as plants. Some may live their entire lives as rooted roses; a seedling becomes Rosavyn only if Vaelora Kindles it. A family lineage may therefore include both mobile Rosavyn and rooted rose descendants, making ancestral gardens quite literally family gardens.
Exact male/female reproductive roles are intentionally unresolved. Real roses often contain both male and female floral structures, so Rosavyn reproduction will be designed from botanical biology rather than forcing mammalian mechanics onto a rose.
Signature Talent — Briarhold
Under severe threat, a Rosavyn can redirect stored resources into rapid extension and interlocking of existing young canes, forming a temporary living briar enclosure. It costs water and stored carbohydrates. Repeated use can suppress flowering, exhaust the individual, or cause cane dieback. Heroics, regrettably, still require calories.
Vulnerabilities
Fire becomes especially dangerous as woody tissue accumulates. Drought limits regeneration and bloom. Persistent fungal disease can strip vigor from the whole organism, heavy shade reduces growth, and overinvestment in defense steals resources from reproduction and fruiting.
Developmental Stages
Rosavyn development begins as ordinary Rosa-line life: Seed → Germination/Seedling. Most unkindled seedlings continue as ordinary rooted plants. A selected seedling experiences the Kindling, then passes through Transforming Kindled Seedling → Seedform → Infant → Mid Stage → Adult. The accepted Seedform, Infant, Mid Stage, and Adult references now anchor the developmental direction; ordinary germination, the Kindling event, and the transforming seedling remain to be visually established.
Official Visual Record
Rosavyn visual canon now includes Official Image Card 001 for the adult male and female, plus accepted Mid Stage female, Mid Stage male, Infant male, and Infant female reference cards. Together they establish a continuous developmental direction from infancy through adulthood. Female dominant blooms develop at the crown/head; male dominant blooms develop at the shoulder, shoulder blade, or upper back. Seedling remains the only major Rosavyn developmental stage not yet visually established. Earlier exploratory Rosavyn generations remain non-canon.
